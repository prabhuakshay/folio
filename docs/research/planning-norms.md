# Indian personal-finance planning norms

Research for [#3](https://github.com/prabhuakshay/folio/issues/3), feeding the Folio doctrine discussion. It collects facts and ranges and does not choose any doctrine. Researched 2026-09-30.

**How to read the labels.** Each norm is tagged with how binding it is:

- **Regulation**: a rule a regulator enforces on regulated entities. It still does not bind an individual's own plan.
- **Regulator education**: guidance or worked examples in material published by a regulator (SEBI, NCFE). It is not binding.
- **Industry practice**: what lenders or insurers actually do under their own board-approved policies.
- **Convention**: a planner rule of thumb with no Indian regulatory source.
- **Research**: a study or data series.

## 1. Emergency fund

- **No Indian regulator states a months-of-expenses number as a rule.**
- SEBI's *Financial Education Booklet* (Nov 2020) has a SMART-goal worked example: save "sufficient amount to fund six months of my living expenses". SEBI's *Investment Planning for Retired People* booklet repeats the "six months of my living expenses" example. — [SEBI FE Booklet p.12](https://investor.sebi.gov.in/pdf/downloadable-documents/Financial%20Education%20Booklet%20-%20English.pdf), [SEBI retired-people booklet](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/investors/financial_literacy/Investment%20Planning%20for%20Retired%20People.pdf)
- NCFE's savings leaflet (NCFE is promoted jointly by RBI, SEBI, IRDAI and PFRDA) says "Always have an Emergency Fund" and "top it up again as soon as you can". It gives no size. — [NCFE fl-savings.pdf](https://ncfe.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/fl-savings.pdf)
- The NISM Investment Adviser Level 1 syllabus teaches liquidity ratios and contingency planning, but the public test objectives give no number. The workbook itself is paid and was not read. — [NISM test objectives](https://www.nism.ac.in/test-objectives-investment-adviser-level-1/)
- **Convention.** Indian planners and AMCs use 3–6 months of essential or non-discretionary expenses, and often 6–12 months for self-employed people or those with variable income. This is the same rule of thumb as in US planning. No primary Indian source pins the range.

## 2. Term-life and health cover

**Term life**
- **IRDAI sets no cover multiple.** IRDAI states: "The Authority has not issued any Guidelines/Circular/rules regarding the eligibility to take term insurance or income/education/age criteria for availing term insurance … Life Insurers issue Insurance policies based on their board approved underwriting policy." Insurers use income proof to cap "disproportionately high sum assured". — [IRDAI Life FAQ](https://irdai.gov.in/life3)
- **Regulator education:** "It is generally recommended to purchase an insurance with coverage worth 7 to 10 times your annual income." — [NCFE Financial Education Handbook, 2nd ed. 2021](https://ncfe.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/FE_Handbook_Eng.pdf)
- **Industry practice.** Insurers' financial-underwriting grids fall by age. An ICICI Prudential manual, as indexed in search results but not retrieved directly, shows 25× income up to age 35 and 20× for ages 36–45. That is a ceiling on what insurers will sell, not a recommendation. — [ICICI Pru underwriting guidelines](https://www.iciciprulife.com/Digitisation/Resource_Center/process-guide/Underwriting/underwriting%20guidelines.pdf)
- **Convention.** Planners use a Human Life Value or income-replacement calculation: the present value of future income net of personal expenses, plus liabilities, minus existing assets. Rules of thumb run 10–15× and sometimes up to 20×. Some sites claim IRDAI "formally recognises" HLV. **This was not verified against any IRDAI document.**

**Health**
- **No regulator multiple or minimum.** IRDAI's standard product, Arogya Sanjeevani, has a floor of ₹50,000 of sum insured in ₹50,000 steps and no upper cap, after IRDAI removed the old ₹1–5 lakh band. That is a product rule, not an adequacy norm. — [IRDAI Arogya Sanjeevani FAQ](https://irdai.gov.in/faq-s-on-standard-health-product-arogya-sanjeevani-product)
- NCFE's handbook says hospital bills "can severely impact your savings" and advises getting insured. It gives no amount. — [NCFE FE Handbook](https://ncfe.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/FE_Handbook_Eng.pdf)
- **Research, needed if cover is to be inflation-indexed.** Medical cost trend for 2026 is 11.5% (Aon, down from 13% for 2025) and 12.9% gross (WTW). These are employer group-plan trends, not a CPI series. — [Aon 2026](https://www.aon.com/apac/in-the-press/asia-newsroom/2025/india-outpaces-global-average-with-2026-medical-trend-at-11-5-percent-aon-reports), [WTW 2026](https://www.wtwco.com/en-in/insights/2025/12/asia-pacific-medical-inflation-continues-to-soar-in-2026)
- **Convention.** Common advice is a family floater of ₹5–10 lakh or more (higher in metros) plus a super top-up. This is not sourced to any authority.

## 3. Debt-to-income and EMI-to-income

- **Regulation, narrow.** For microfinance loans, meaning collateral-free loans to households with annual income up to ₹3,00,000, lenders must cap "outflows on account of repayment of monthly loan obligations of a household as a percentage of the monthly household income" at **a maximum of 50%**. This is the only hard EMI-to-income cap in Indian regulation that was found. — [RBI Master Direction, Microfinance Loans 2022](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12256), [RBI FAQ](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/FAQs/PDFs/RFML30012025.pdf)
- **Regulation, a collateral limit rather than an income limit.** Home-loan loan-to-value caps are 90% for loans up to ₹30 lakh, 80% for ₹30–75 lakh and 75% above ₹75 lakh. — [RBI LTV](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=1269)
- **Industry practice.** RBI does not mandate a FOIR (fixed obligations to income ratio) for retail loans. Banks usually approve at a FOIR of about 40–55% of net income, and higher for high earners. — e.g. [BankBazaar FOIR](https://www.bankbazaar.com/personal-loan/fixed-obligations-to-income-ratio-foir.html) (secondary)
- **Convention.** Planners often say to keep a home-loan EMI at or below 30–35% of take-home pay and total EMIs at or below about 40%. This mirrors the US 28/36 rule. No Indian authority states it.
- NISM teaches a "Debt to Income Ratio" but its public objectives give no benchmark. — [NISM](https://www.nism.ac.in/test-objectives-investment-adviser-level-1/)
- **Context.** Household financial liabilities were 4.8% of GNDI in FY25, down from 6.4% in FY24. Net household financial savings were 7.0% of GNDI. — [RBI Annual Report via Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/net-household-financial-savings-rise-to-7-of-gndi-in-fy25-rbi-report-126052901658_1.html)

## 4. Savings rate

- **Regulator education**, from SEBI's *Investment Planning for Retired People*: "It is hard to apply a rule of thumb toward savings, because it varies with age and income level. **Ten percent is a good start.**" It then says: "Rule of thumb ideally says that you need to save **20% of your income**." — [SEBI booklet](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/investors/financial_literacy/Investment%20Planning%20for%20Retired%20People.pdf)
- The same booklet gives a retirement income replacement rule of thumb: "replace **70 to 90 percent** of your pre-retirement income". Its worked example uses 80% of present expenses.
- **Convention.** 50/30/20 (needs/wants/savings) is a US heuristic. It is widely repeated in India but has no Indian regulatory source.

## 5. Horizon- and age-based equity:debt allocation

**SEBI goal horizons (regulator education).** SEBI's booklet classifies goals as short term (a few months to 1 year), medium term (1–8 years) and long term (8+ years). Its example mixes of equity/debt/gold are 70/15/15, 40/50/10 and 60/25/15. — [SEBI FE Booklet pp.12–13](https://investor.sebi.gov.in/pdf/downloadable-documents/Financial%20Education%20Booklet%20-%20English.pdf)

**SEBI retirement glide example (regulator education).** SEBI's retired-people booklet shows this "asset mix by years to retirement" (stocks/bonds/cash):

| Years to retirement | Stocks | Bonds | Cash |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 80% | 10% | 10% |
| 15 | 60% | 30% | 10% |
| 10 | 40% | 50% | 10% |
| 5 | 30% | 60% | 10% |

**NPS lifecycle funds (regulation on the product; age-based).** The equity share of each fund is below. Each fund starts tapering at 36 (BLC at 46) and flattens from age 55. The source also gives the corporate-bond and G-sec split for every year of age. — [NPS CRA: Investment options under NPS](https://www.npscra.proteantech.in/download/Investment_options_under_NPS_02.pdf)

| Age | LC75 (E) | LC50 (E) | LC25 (E) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≤35 | 75 | 50 | 25 |
| 40 | 55 | 40 | 20 |
| 45 | 35 | 30 | 15 |
| 50 | 20 | 20 | 10 |
| ≥55 | 15 | 10 | 5 |

- LC75 falls 4 points a year from 36 to 45, then 3 points a year to 50, then 1 point a year. LC50 falls 2 points a year. LC25 falls 1 point a year.
- **Balanced Life Cycle (BLC)** (circular PFRDA/2024/17/PDES/02, 1 Oct 2024) holds 50% equity until age 45 and tapers to 35% by 55. — [PFRDA BLC circular](https://www.pfrda.org.in/web/pfrda/w/regulatory-framework/circulars/active-circulars/introduction-of-balanced-life-cycle-fund-under-nps). The per-year table was not extracted because the PDF is scanned.
- LC75 and BLC were extended to central-government NPS/UPS subscribers in 2025. — [PFRDA/PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2182253&reg=48&lang=2)
- PFRDA then renamed the lifecycle funds because BLC held *more* equity at 45 and 55 than the "aggressive" LC75. — [PFRDA rationalisation note](https://pfrda.org.in/documents/33652/240154/1Rationalization+of+Names+and+Structure+of+Auto+Choice++Life+Cycle+Funds+under+NPS.pdf). A secondary summary gives the new names (circular PFRDA/2026/47/REG-PF/10, 28 Aug 2026) as Aggressive for BLC, High for LC75, Moderate for LC50 and Low for LC25. **This is secondary and not verified against the PFRDA table.** — [staffnews](https://www.staffnews.in/2026/09/standardised-framework-under-the-nps-pfrda.html)
- NPS Active Choice caps equity at 75% in Tier I and 100% in Tier II. — [NPS CRA](https://www.npscra.proteantech.in/download/Investment_options_under_NPS_02.pdf)
- Since 1 Oct 2025, the NPS Multiple Scheme Framework lets non-government subscribers choose "high-risk" schemes with up to 100% equity, subject to a 15-year vesting period. — [PFRDA circular PFRDA/2025/09/REG-PF/01](https://pfrda.org.in/documents/33652/154008/Circular+on+Introduction+of+MSF+for+Non+govt+Sector+subscribers+under+NPS.pdf)

**Convention.** Rules like "100 − age" (or 110/120 − age) in equity are US-origin heuristics with no Indian regulatory source. They are more aggressive than NPS LC75 after age 40: at 45, the rule gives 55% equity against LC75's 35%.

## 6. Glide path toward a goal date

This is the most directly adoptable primary source for Folio's per-Goal Target Allocation.

**SEBI Life Cycle Funds (regulation, 26 Feb 2026).** Circular HO/24/13/15(2)2026-IMD-RAC4/I/5764/2026 created a Life Cycle Fund category, which is target-date, 5–30 years in multiples of 5, and named e.g. "Life Cycle Fund 2045". It also **discontinued solution-oriented (retirement and children's) schemes.** Its Annexure B mandates allocation bands by *years to maturity*. The bands are the same whatever the fund's starting tenure. — [SEBI circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/feb-2026/categorization-and-rationalization-of-mutual-fund-schemes_99983.html) ([PDF](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/feb-2026/1772079826878.pdf)); later consolidated as clause 3.7 of the [MF Master Circular (Mar 2026)](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/master-circulars/mar-2026/master-circular-for-mutual-funds_100491.html).

| Years to maturity | Equity | Debt | Gold/Silver ETFs, ETCDs, InvITs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15–30 | 65–95% | 5–25% | 0–10% |
| 10–15 | 65–80% | 5–25% | 0–10% |
| 5–10 | 50–65% | 5–25% | 0–10% |
| 3–5 | 35–50% | 25–50% | 0–10% |
| 1–3 | 20–35% | 25–65%* | 0–10% |
| < 1 | 5–20% | 25–65%* | 0–10% |

\* Debt limited to AA-and-above instruments maturing before the target date. With under 5 years to go, funds may add up to 50% in equity arbitrage. Exit loads of 3%/2%/1% apply within years 1/2/3.

**Other glide paths for comparison:** the SEBI retirement booklet example (§5, 80% equity at 20 years out to 30% at 5), and NPS lifecycle funds, which are keyed to age rather than goal date.

## 7. Inflation and asset-class return assumptions

**Inflation**
- **Regulation.** CPI inflation target of **4% with a 2–6% tolerance band for 1 Apr 2026 – 31 Mar 2031**, notified 25 Mar 2026 under RBI Act §45ZA. This is the third consecutive five-year period at 4%. — [UNI](http://www.uniindia.com/news/india/government-sets-4-inflation-target-for-2026-2031-period/3787209.html), [BIS/RBI speech](https://www.bis.org/review/r260506b.pdf), [RBI Act Ch. IIIF](http://dea.gov.in/files/acts_documents/Chapter_IIIF_RBI_Act.pdf)
- **Realised inflation was higher.** Average inflation was 7.0% over 1979–2020 (HDFC MF using RBI Handbook data). Mean annual inflation was 6.8% over Dec 1992 – Aug 2024 (Raju). — [HDFC MF Investment Mantra May 2020](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/2020-06/Investment%20Mantra-May%202020.pdf), [Raju, Long-Term Asset Returns in India 1992–2024](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5026391.pdf?abstractid=5026391&mirid=1) (abstract-level)
- **Assumptions in regulator education material:**
  - The NCFE new-entrants handbook (2024) uses a 7% inflation example, an 8% post-retirement return, retirement at 60 and life expectancy of 80. — [NCFE handbook p.49–50](https://ncfe.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/FE-Handbook-for-New-Entrants-at-Workplace.pdf)
  - The SEBI retired-people booklet uses a 5% inflation example, a 12% return and life expectancy of 83. Another of its examples uses 8% inflation.

**Returns (nominal unless stated)**

| Series | Period | CAGR | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensex TRI | Apr 1979 – Mar 2020 | 15.9% | HDFC MF (BSE/Bloomberg) |
| Gold | 1979–2020 | 10.6% | same |
| Bank FD | 1979–2020 | 8.2% | same |
| Equity, real | 1992–2023 | 6.0% (5.4–6.6) | Raju, SSRN 4641106 (abstract) |
| FD, real | 1992–2023 | 1.0% (0.6–1.2) | same |

- **Administered rates** give a floor for debt assumptions. PPF is 7.1%, SCSS and SSY 8.2%, and NSC 7.7% for Jul–Sep 2026, unchanged for nine quarters. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/investment/story/govt-keeps-small-savings-interest-rates-unchanged-for-july-september-quarter-check-latest-ppf-scss-ssy-rates-540090-2026-06-30). The EPF rate for FY26 is 8.25%. — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2234502&reg=3&lang=1)
- **The NPS Trust pension calculator** defaults the expected return to each asset class's trailing 10-year CAGR. It defaults the annuity rate to the prevailing rate for a 60-year-old buying life annuity with return of purchase price. — [NPS Trust calculator manual](https://npstrust.org.in/sites/default/files/NPS_Pension_Calculator_User_Manual.pdf)
- **NPS exit, which affects decumulation modelling.** Since the Dec 2025 amendments, non-government subscribers may take up to 80% as a lump sum at normal exit, with at least 20% going to an annuity (previously 60/40). A corpus of ₹8 lakh or less can be withdrawn fully. — [PFRDA press release 19 Dec 2025](https://www.pfrda.org.in/documents/33652/86710/Press+Release+-+Key+changes+-+Exit+Regulations+.pdf)
- **Longevity.** SRS life expectancy at birth for 2017–21 was 69.8 years. The at-age-60 figure was not retrieved. Education examples plan to age 80–83. — [SRS abridged life tables](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/45558)

## 8. Safe withdrawal rate evidence for India

- **Saraogi (2022)**, *Computing the Safe Withdrawal Rate for a Retirement Portfolio in India* (SSRN 4216077). It applies Bengen's method and simulation on adjusted data, and finds India's SWR is below 4%: "**3 percent is suitable**" for an average investor and "**no more than 2.6 percent**" for a risk-conservative one. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4216077)
- **Raju & Saraogi (2024)**, *Balancing Acts* (SSRN 4697720). It runs Monte Carlo and circular-bootstrap simulations over equity, FD and gold, with 25/30/35-year horizons, annual rebalancing, and tax of 30% on FDs and 10% on equity. Findings:
  - The 4% rule is "not suitable"; **3.0–3.5%** "emerges as more appropriate".
  - SWR first rises with equity share and then falls. **40–50% equity** looks appropriate.
  - Adding gold improves withdrawal rates.
  - The low SWR is caused by high structural inflation plus volatile returns.
  - Source: [Raju, Institute of Actuaries of India TechTalk slides, Nov 2024](https://www.actuariesindia.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/Presentation%20for%2012th%20Techtalk%20on%20Employee%20Benefits.pdf)
- **Raju (2025)**, *Safe Withdrawal Rates in India … (1992–2024)* (SSRN 5114252). It confirms the 4% rule is too generic for India, especially after the Budget 2025 tax changes. It finds moderate equity (20–50%) gives consistently higher SWRs, and it compares fixed-real, dynamic and declining spending rules. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5114252.pdf?abstractid=5114252) (abstract-level)
- **Annuity cross-check** (same slides): current Indian annuity rates price ₹1 of level (non-increasing) pension at about 12×, against about 22× for an actuarially inflation-linked pension. Increasing annuities are "conspicuously missing" in India.
- **No regulator publishes an SWR.**

## Open points for the doctrine discussion

- The two strongest primary anchors for Target Allocation are the SEBI Life Cycle bands (keyed to years to goal date) and the NPS lifecycle tables (keyed to age). They disagree for retirement: SEBI allows 65–95% equity at 15+ years out, while NPS LC75 is at 35% by age 45.
- Folio needs an inflation default. The choice is between RBI's 4% target, realised ~6.8–7%, and the 5–7% used in regulator education. Health goals may need their own medical-trend rate of about 11–13%.
- India's research consensus on SWR (3–3.5%) is materially lower than the widely quoted 4%. The same research favours moderate equity (40–50%) *during* withdrawal, which is more than NPS LC tables hold at 55+ (5–15%).
- The EMI-to-income cap (50%) and the insurance multiples (7–10×) are the only regulator-sourced numbers for their topics, and each is narrow. Everything else here is convention.
